# Prospects: Insurance, health tech, fintech/lending, HR and education SaaS

Researched 2026-10-04. 20 companies. 19 have at least one public source showing in-app document viewing, highlighting, annotation, click-to-source review or signing. Tennr does not, and is kept only as an interview target (fit 1). Machine-readable version: `regulated.csv` (same rows).

## How to read this

- **Fit (1-5)** measures how well a company matches what Margin can sell today: a web viewer with highlights, ink, shapes, sticky notes, comment threads, drawn signatures and search, at $5k-$12k a year. A company scores lower if it is large, likely built its viewer in-house, or mainly needs things the demo does not have yet: fillable form fields, signature-field tagging, true redaction, full PDF text editing or accessibility features like read-aloud.
- **Confidence** is about the facts in the row. High means a vendor case study or the company's own docs say it directly. Medium means a search snippet of the company's or a university's page describes the PDF feature, but I could not open the page. Low means the PDF viewer is inferred from "click to source" or "document review" language.
- **SDK "Unknown"** means I found no public evidence. I have not guessed.
- **Roles** are titles only. No individuals were researched.

Every fact below comes from search-result snippets of the linked pages. Treat them as accurate summaries, not verified quotes. Open the URL before quoting it to a prospect.

- Job-post searches for PDFTron/Apryse, PSPDFKit/Nutrient or PDF.js at companies in these segments returned nothing useful. GitHub code search for `@pdftron/webviewer` showed only samples and hobby projects.
- Three SDK links are confirmed, all from Nutrient: Current (case study) and Faria Education Group and Texthelp (logos on Nutrient's education solutions page, read through a search snippet). Apryse's customer pages could not be read. Someone with a normal browser should check apryse.com/customers, apryse.com/industries/insurance and apryse.com/industries/healthcare, plus nutrient.io/sdk/solutions/healthcare and /financial-services. That is the fastest way to add high-confidence rows.
- HR is thin. I found no mid-size HR SaaS with public evidence of in-app PDF markup beyond e-signing forms. Only one HR row is included.

## Notes by segment

**Insurance (Wisedocs, DigitalOwl, Sixfold, Indico).** All four sell AI over long documents and promise traceability back to the source page. That makes the viewer and the highlight layer central to their trust story. Wisedocs is the strongest fit: mid-size, Series A, and its own materials describe an in-platform PDF viewer with annotation, tags and comments. DigitalOwl and Wisedocs also sell to law firms, which overlaps with the legal list. Lead with "documents never leave the browser" and "no per-page or per-record fees". Medical record volumes make metered pricing painful.

**Health tech (Carta Healthcare, IntakeQ, Cohere, Tennr).** The evidence is weaker here. Most products cite source documents but say little about how they display them. Carta Healthcare is the best bet: Series B1 in 2025, and abstractors read citations in context. Ask in discovery whether the sources are PDFs or EHR HTML. IntakeQ needs fillable form fields, which the demo lacks. Tennr has no public evidence of in-app document viewing and is an interview target only.

**Fintech and lending (Inscribe, Current, Snapdocs, Floify, Ocrolus).** Inscribe is the cleanest target: 44 people, Series B, and the core UI is a document with highlighted regions. Current is a confirmed Nutrient customer that values speed with a tiny team, which makes it a good renewal-time conversation. But it also uses Nutrient's editing and forms, so the pitch has to be honest about scope. Snapdocs and Ocrolus are large. Treat them as interviews about volume pricing. Floify is mostly forms and e-sign and may sit inside a larger parent company, so it moved down to fit 2.

**HR (WorkBright).** Only one row. HR onboarding tools mostly need form filling and e-sign, not markup. Margin's drawn signatures fit, form fields do not. Low priority until form fields ship.

**Education (Faria, Crowdmark, Perusall, FeedbackFruits, Texthelp, Kami).** The feature match is the closest of any segment: pen, highlighter, stamps and threaded comments are the grading and social-reading workflow. The catch is budget. Crowdmark and Perusall have about 16 employees each and little or no outside funding, so $5k a year is a real decision for them. Faria is the most interesting: a Nutrient logo (seen via search snippet) and several products under one group, which makes the per-app pricing story relevant.

## What not to say

- Do not tell Faria, Texthelp or Current that we know their contract terms. We only know they appear on Nutrient's site.
- Do not claim form filling, signature-field tagging, redaction or read-aloud. The demo has none of these.
- Do not claim HIPAA, SOC 2 or customers. Client-side rendering keeps documents in the browser. That is true of the demo and is the honest privacy point.
