# Prospects: Construction, AEC, real estate and field-service SaaS

Researched 2026-10-04. 19 companies. Each has at least one public source showing in-app PDF viewing, markup, pins, signing or review of drawings, plans, submittals, contracts or forms. Trunk Tools and INGENIOUS.BUILD show viewing only, not markup, and are scored to match. Machine-readable version: `construction.csv` (same rows).

## How to read this

- **Fit (1-5)** measures how well a company matches what Margin can sell today: a web viewer with annotations, comment threads, signatures and search, at $5k-$12k a year. A company scores lower if it is very large, mostly native mobile, or needs features the demo lacks.
- **Confidence** is about the facts in the row. High means a vendor case study names the company and the SDK. Medium means the company's own docs, blog or help center show the PDF feature, but the SDK is unknown. Low means the PDF markup use is inferred from product descriptions.
- **SDK "Unknown"** means I found no public evidence. I have not guessed.
- **Roles** are titles only. No individuals were researched.

## The segment-specific gap to know before outreach

Construction buyers ask for things legal buyers rarely do. The demo does **not** have:

1. **Measurement and scale calibration** (length, area, count on a scaled sheet). Every takeoff tool (Togal.AI, Buildxact, STACK) and most plan-review tools (e-PlanSoft) need it.
2. **Sheet compare / overlay** of two drawing revisions (Fieldwire, Togal.AI, Drawboard).
3. **Proven performance on very large vector sheets** (E-size drawings, 500+ page sets). This hasn't been tested. Benchmark the demo on a real plan set before using any speed claim.
4. **Native iOS/Android SDKs.** Three of the four confirmed SDK buyers (Capmo, CMiC, Trinoor) use Nutrient on native mobile.
5. **CAD/BIM** formats.

Be upfront about these on calls. Our strongest offer here is review and collaboration on PDFs: markup, comment threads on a sheet, pins, signatures, Acrobat-compatible export. That suits submittal review, document review and simple plan markup better than takeoff. Measurement is the most likely feature to come up in interviews. Log each request as roadmap evidence.

## Method and limits

- Sources: web search, plus company help centers, product-update pages, funding announcements and vendor case studies quoted in search snippets.
- The network proxy blocked direct fetches of apryse.com, pspdfkit.com, nutrient.io and several data aggregators. Facts from those sites come from search-result snippets of the linked pages. Open the URL before quoting it to a prospect.
- I found no public job posts at construction-tech companies naming PDFTron/Apryse, PSPDFKit/Nutrient or PDF.js. All four high-confidence SDK rows come from vendor case studies.
- Size figures come from aggregators (Getlatka, Growjo, CB Insights, ZoomInfo) or press releases, and are rough. "Not verified" means I did not find a number I trusted.
- Apryse publicly lists Bentley, Autodesk, Trimble and Aconex as AEC customers. They are left out because they are far too large and slow for a founding-customer program.

## Notes per company

- **Drawboard** (4/5, high for the Apryse relationship). Confirmed Apryse customer in our exact segment and a small team, so SDK cost is a real share of spend. Whether Projects (the web app) runs on Apryse WebViewer is not confirmed; check the network tab before the call. The old hook quoted the case study's line about a bad experience with a previous SDK. That read as too familiar for a cold opener, so it was replaced. Fit is 4, not 5: their Windows app runs on Apryse Core and Margin is web-only. Treat the web viewer in Projects as the realistic wedge.
- **e-PlanSoft** (4/5, medium). Browser markup is the core product and the company is small, so build-vs-buy cost matters. The gap: they advertise measurement, and Margin has no measurement or scale calibration today.
- **Part3** (4/5, medium). Early stage, web-first, and the product is reviewing PDFs (submittals and cut sheets), which is close to what the demo already does. Budget may be tight. The founding-customer price ($2.5k/yr on Startup) is the pitch.
- **Trunk Tools** (3/5, low; was 4/5, medium). Funded, and drawings are a main UI surface. But the only source is a funding article describing a drawing viewer with linked RFIs and specs. Nothing shows markup or annotation, so this was downgraded. If a demo or help doc shows users annotating drawings, move it back to fit 4. 'No metering' is still the right hook for an AI product that ingests a lot of documents.
- **Document Crunch** (4/5, medium). Text highlighting and comments on contract PDFs fit the demo well. Their AI review runs on their servers, so 'documents never leave the browser' doesn't help them and was taken out of the hook. Nemetschek (owner of Bluebeam) is a strategic investor, which could push them toward Bluebeam tech. Ask about this early.
- **JobTread** (4/5, medium). Small team that keeps shipping markup features, which suggests active investment. The tool set (shapes, arrows, text, callouts) matches the demo almost one to one. If they built it on raw PDF.js, the pitch shifts to maintenance cost.
- **Togal.AI** (3/5, low; was medium because the only evidence is a third-party tool directory, not Togal's own site). Heavy web PDF use, but takeoff needs scale calibration, measurement and vector snapping. Margin does not have these. Pitch the viewer/markup layer as a foundation and be honest about the gap.
- **Buildxact** (3/5, medium). The help doc reportedly caps plan files at 20 pages, which hints at a rendering or cost constraint worth asking about. Confirm the cap on the live help page before mentioning it; the hook was rewritten so it doesn't depend on it. Same measurement gap as Togal.
- **Kahua** (3/5, medium). Clear in-app markup. Government buyers make 'documents never leave the browser' useful. Larger company with a longer sales cycle. Enterprise tier likely.
- **Newforma** (3/5, medium). The web markup feature matches the demo closely, including export to PDF. PE ownership often means cost scrutiny on vendors, which helps us. Older codebase, so switching costs are higher.
- **STACK Construction Technologies** (3/5, medium). Free tier plus a 100% cloud viewer makes metered SDK pricing painful, which suits our positioning. Same measurement gap as other takeoff tools.
- **PlanRadar** (3/5, medium). Plan-centric product with an exact match to pins plus export. Mostly mobile-first, and large enough that they may have built their own. Ask what renders plans on web today.
- **Capmo** (3/5, high). Confirmed SDK buyer in-segment, but the case study is native mobile and Margin is web-only. Worth a conversation about the web app and about their Nutrient renewal experience, even if it is only an interview.
- **Fieldwire (Hilti)** (3/5, medium). Heavy PDF use, but now inside Hilti's procurement, and features like sheet compare go beyond Margin today. Good for learning; slow to close.
- **SkySlope (Forms)** (3/5, medium). Strong fit on highlight, strikeout and text. Real form filling and true redaction are needed, and the demo has neither yet. Real estate budgets can be tight.
- **Archistar** (3/5, low). PDF review is part of the workflow, but much of the value is in CAD/BIM and 3D, which Margin doesn't cover. Low confidence that a PDF SDK is a big line item.
- **INGENIOUS.BUILD** (2/5, low). Series A, funded, real-estate-side buyer. Evidence of actual markup (not just viewing) is weak. Confirm in the first conversation before investing outreach time.
- **CMiC** (2/5, high). Confirmed SDK buyer, but enterprise size, mobile-focused, and the existing vendor relationship is deep. Best used as a research interview on SDK pain, not a near-term sale.
- **Trinoor** (2/5, high). Confirmed SDK user in field service, but native mobile and an adjacent vertical (energy, not construction). Interview candidate.

## Where to start

1. **First wave (fit 4):** Drawboard, e-PlanSoft, Part3, Document Crunch, JobTread. (Trunk Tools moved to the second tier until there is evidence of markup.) Small or mid-size teams, web-first, and their PDF work is review and markup more than measurement. Drawboard is the only one with a confirmed SDK vendor (Apryse), so ask them directly about their renewal experience.
2. **Interview, not sale (high-confidence SDK buyers, weaker fit):** Capmo, CMiC, Trinoor. They pay Nutrient today and can tell us exactly what they pay for and what annoys them. That is worth more than a cold pitch.
3. **Takeoff tools (Togal.AI, Buildxact, STACK):** hold off until we decide whether measurement is on the roadmap. Otherwise the first demo ends at "where's the scale tool?"
4. **Checks to run with a normal browser:** read apryse.com/customers, apryse.com/industries/aec, and nutrient.io/blog/categories/customer-stories for more named AEC customers. Look in BuiltWith or the network tab of each prospect's public web app for `webviewer`, `pspdfkit` or `pdf.worker` files. That turns medium-confidence rows into high-confidence ones quickly.

## Considered and excluded

- **Procore** (public, very large), **Autodesk Construction Cloud / PlanGrid** (Autodesk; PlanGrid help docs mention PSPDFKit for Field Reports), **Bluebeam** (Nemetschek; builds its own engine), **Trimble, Bentley, Aconex/Oracle** (listed Apryse customers, but far too large).
- **Avolve ProjectDox** (leading e-plan review; no public evidence of SDK or company size found in this pass, worth a second look).
- **Chandler Concrete** and **DB Systel** (Nutrient case studies, but they build apps for internal use, not for sale).
- **PermitFlow, Snapdocs, CompanyCam** (well-funded and adjacent, but I found no clear evidence of in-app PDF markup. CompanyCam annotates scanned documents with photo tools only).
